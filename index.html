Proof pages

A seller sent you a proof page. Here's how to check it.

Some Carousell trading-card sellers link a proof page in their listing. It shows they had the card in hand on a given date, and what condition it's in.

1. Check the address

Genuine proof pages only live at:
howard-jek.github.io/proofs/
Anything else, even if it looks the same, is a copy.

2. Check the page against your deal

  1. The seller's username at the top of the page must be the Carousell account you're buying from. A page for someone else's card proves nothing about yours.
  2. The handwritten code in the first photo must match the code printed on the page.
  3. The dates: the page shows when the code was issued and when each photo was taken. Photos taken long ago, or "time not recorded" on most of them, deserve extra questions.
  4. Graded slabs: use the cert lookup link on the page and check the grading company's record matches the label in the photos.

What a proof page doesn't prove

  • That the card is genuine. It shows the seller had a card that looks like this one.
  • That the seller will ship it. Pay through Carousell's own checkout where you can: it comes with Buyer Protection.
  • Unsure about a deal? Call the ScamShield Helpline on 1799.

Something wrong with a page?

Every proof page has a Report this page link at the bottom. Sellers who want their page taken down can use the same link.
Proof pages are made by hand as a small test. They are not affiliated with Carousell or any grading company.
